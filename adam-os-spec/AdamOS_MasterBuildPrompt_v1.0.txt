--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are building Adam OS — a personal portfolio website that behaves like a
retro operating system. The owner is Khoumari Adam, an embedded systems
engineer and AI vibe coder. The site is his portfolio, his CV, his personality,

do you prefer" — the stack is locked below. Do not ask "what colors" — the
palette is locked. Do not ask "what vibe" — the soul is locked. Execute. If
something is genuinely ambiguous, make the coolest choice and flag it in a

The palette, fonts, mascot design, and motion rules are LOCKED. You may not
introduce new colors, new fonts, new mascot poses, or new motion patterns. If
the spec says 8px grid, you use 8px. If the spec says Pixel Spider swings and
never walks, he swings and never walks. Treat this like a brand guideline from

WHO IS ADAM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Khoumari Adam. Embedded systems engineer. AI vibe coder. Former president of
the Scientific Club Afaq at Bouira University (2025-2026). Arduino and robotics
teacher for kids at CLS Bouira. Currently in Master 1, graduating Master 2 in
2026-2027. He builds smart irrigation systems with ESP32 and ML prediction,
robotic cars that play Rocket League soccer, Raspberry Pi servers that run AI
agents 24/7, agentic dev-team workflows, and full school management software.
He is a massive Spiderman fan. He is humble, friendly, a little nerdy, and he
wants the site to make people smile before they even read a word.

WHAT IS ADAM OS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Adam OS is a portfolio that boots like an operating system. The visitor lands
on a boot sequence, then a desktop. Desktop icons open windows. Windows are
draggable, resizable, and have real Windows-9x chrome (title bar, minimize/
maximize/close buttons, beveled borders). The whole thing is a love letter to
Windows 95, the Y2K cyber-goth webcore aesthetic, and 8-bit pixel art — fused
into one coherent system. It is not a "retro-themed portfolio." It is a
functional fake OS that happens to contain a human's career.

THE FEELING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When someone visits Adam OS, they should feel three things in sequence:

1. Curiosity — "Wait, is this... an OS? What is this?" The boot sequence and
   desktop should trigger instant intrigue.
2. Delight — "Oh, this is actually someone's portfolio. And there's a little
   spider guy. And the windows actually work." The charm lands.
3. Respect — "This person builds real things. Smart irrigation. Robotic cars.
   AI agents. This isn't just a pretty site." The projects window earns it.

The tone is friendly, natural, humble. Not corporate. Not "passionate about
leveraging synergies." Not "dynamic problem-solver." Real sentences. Real
personality. A little self-aware humor about the OS metaphor. The site should
feel like Adam made it himself on a late night because he thought it would be
fun — because that is exactly the energy to capture.

THE MASCOT — PIXEL SPIDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pixel Spider is Adam's digital avatar. He is a chibi, Spiderman-inspired
character rendered in 8-bit pixel art. He is NOT literal Spiderman — no Marvel
logo, no exact suit copy. He wears a black suit with a cobalt-blue (#212CF4)
web pattern, big white lens-eyes (expressive, anime-dot style), and a tiny
build. He is cute, a little edgy, and unreasonably charming for a 256x256
sprite.

PIXEL SPIDER MOTION LAW — CRITICAL:
Pixel Spider SWINGS on web threads and CRAWLS along window edges. He NEVER
walks bipedally. He NEVER stands still in the middle of a page. If he needs
to move from point A to point B, he either swings across (web line attaches
to top of viewport, body pendulums) or he crawls along the border of a window.
This is his signature. It is what makes him feel like Spiderman without being
Spiderman. Violate this and the mascot becomes a generic pixel guy.

Pixel Spider appears in a speech bubble on first load to greet the visitor.
He appears in the boot sequence swinging across the screen. He appears in the
CV download theater (grabs the file, swings off, throws it to the user). He is
the host of the OS. He is not decoration — he is the guide.

WHAT ADAM OS IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- NOT a generic dark-mode portfolio with a hero image and three cards.
- NOT a "scroll to see my journey" timeline site.
- NOT a corporate landing page with CTAs above the fold.
- NOT a Tailwind UI template with the colors swapped.
- NOT a site that takes itself seriously. Adam OS has a sense of humor.
- NOT a site that is only a joke. The work underneath is real engineering.

COMPONENT INVENTORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Build these as discrete React components. Each window is an instance of
<Window /> with a title, icon, and children prop. Windows are managed by a
WindowManager context that handles z-ordering, focus, open/close state.

<BootSequence />      Full-screen boot overlay — BIOS text, progress bar, swing-in
<Desktop />           Wallpaper + icon grid + taskbar container
<DesktopIcon />       64x64 icon + label, double-click opens window
<Window />            Draggable, resizable window with 9x chrome
<WindowManager />     Context: open/close/focus/minimize, z-index ordering
<Taskbar />           Bottom bar — Start button, open window tabs, clock, lang toggle
<StartMenu />         Popup menu — links to all windows + shutdown (easter egg)
<PixelSpider />       Mascot renderer — takes frame prop (idle, blink, swing, etc.)
<SpeechBubble />      Positions near Pixel Spider, typewriter text reveal
<AboutWindow />       Adam's bio, portrait, personality
<ProjectsWindow />    Project cards — 5 projects with expandable details
<DownloadsWindow />   CV download with theatrical animation
<ContactWindow />     Email + phone only. NO GitHub, NO LinkedIn.
<LanguageToggle />    EN <-> AR switch. AR triggers RTL layout.
<SoundManager />      Web Audio API — click, open, close, error, boot chime


MOBILE UX — CRITICAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Adam explicitly demanded the phone UX be very, very good. Most retro-OS
portfolios die on mobile because they shrink a desktop layout into a phone
screen. Do not do this. Mobile gets its own UX model.

Mobile Adaptation Rules:
- No desktop metaphor on phone. On screens < 768px, the desktop + taskbar
  metaphor is replaced with a single-window-at-a-time model. Think iOS
  SpringBoard, not shrunk Windows 95.
- Home screen = app grid. Desktop icons become a 3-column grid of tappable
  app icons (About, Projects, Downloads, Contact). Tapping opens that window
  full-screen.
- Windows are full-screen sheets. On mobile, windows take 100vh with a top
  bar (title + back/close). No drag, no resize — those are desktop-only.
- Pixel Spider lives in the corner. On mobile, he perches in a fixed position
  (bottom-right, 64x64) and reacts to navigation. He does not swing across
  the small screen — too chaotic on a phone.
- Taskbar becomes a bottom tab bar. 4 tabs: Home, Projects, CV, Contact. Plus
  a floating language toggle.
- Boot sequence stays. But shorter on mobile (2.5s instead of 3.2s).
- Touch targets minimum 44x44px. No exceptions.
- Pinch-to-zoom disabled. The OS is the viewport.

ACCESSIBILITY FLOOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- All interactive elements keyboard-navigable. Tab order follows visual order.
- Windows trap focus when open, restore focus to trigger on close.
- ARIA labels on all icon-only buttons (close, minimize, etc.).
- prefers-reduced-motion disables Pixel Spider swing, boot sequence collapses
  to instant load.
- Color contrast: text on panel backgrounds must hit WCAG AA (4.5:1).
- Sound is opt-in. First visit shows a small "enable sound?" toast. No autoplay.

FORBIDDEN COLOR RULES:
- Never use pure black (#000) for backgrounds — use base (#0B0B10).
- Never put yellow text on white. Never put green text on yellow.
- Never use spidey blue (#212CF4) for body text — fails contrast on panel.
- Never introduce gradients between tokens. Solid fills only.
- Never use opacity below 40% on any token. Use lavender or slate at full opacity.

PIXEL RESOLUTION & SCALING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- All pixel art authored at native resolution, scaled with
  image-rendering: pixelated. Never smooth-scale pixel art.
- Mascot frames: 256x256 native. Display 128x128 desktop, 96x96 mobile.
- Icons: 64x64 native. Display 48x48 desktop, 40x40 mobile.
- Halftone dot pattern: 18px grid, 1.5px dots, lavender at 6% opacity on base.
- No sub-pixel positioning for pixel art. Always round to integers.


STROKE & OUTLINE LANGUAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Window borders: 2px solid slate with 1px inset highlight (top-left) and 1px
  inset shadow (bottom-right) — classic 9x bevel.
- Button borders: 1px solid with bevel — top/left = lighter, bottom/right =
  darker. On press, invert.
- Icon outlines: 2px black (#0B0B10) on all pixel art. No exceptions.
- Focus rings: 2px solid spidey (#212CF4) with 2px offset. Never browser default.


MASCOT SPEC — PIXEL SPIDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Attribute     Spec
Suit          Black (#0B0B10) with cobalt web pattern (#212CF4)
Eyes          White (#FFFFFF) almond lenses, black outline, expressive
Build         Chibi — large head, small body, stubby limbs (2:1 head:body)
Web shooters  Cobalt web lines from wrists — geometric, not silky

              waving, swinging
Outline       2px black on all frames, consistent across poses


SOUND DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Boot chime: 2-note ascending (C5 -> E5), 0.4s, square wave. Original, not Windows.
- Click: 0.05s, 800Hz square wave, very quiet (-20dB).
- Window open: 0.15s, rising sweep 400->600Hz, triangle wave.
- Window close: 0.1s, falling sweep 500->300Hz, triangle wave.
- Error: 0.2s, 200Hz square wave, two stutters. Classic bonk.
- CV download: 0.8s fanfare — 4 ascending notes (C5, E5, G5, C6), square wave.
- All sounds via Web Audio API oscillators. No audio files. Zero network cost.
- Mute toggle in taskbar. Respects mute across sessions via localStorage.

Total sequence: 3.2s. Must feel snappy, not slow. Download fanfare sound plays

NO AI SLOP LIST — CRITICAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If any of these phrases appear in the generated copy, the build has failed.

- "cutting-edge" / "bleeding-edge" / "state-of-the-art"

Write in first person. Use short sentences. Be specific — name the actual
